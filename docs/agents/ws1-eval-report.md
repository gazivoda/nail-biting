# WS1 detection evaluation report

> **SYNTHETIC. This is not real-world accuracy.**
> Every number below comes from 30 landmark fixtures drawn by the same person who wrote the rules (a neutral stick-model face and hand, generated by `scripts/eval-detection/generate-fixtures.mjs`). No real footage has been tried. A high precision here means the rule does what its author imagined on scenes its author drew. It is a guard against regressions and a place to start, nothing more. Do not quote it on the website or anywhere else.

Generated by `npm run eval:detection -- --report docs/agents/ws1-eval-report.md`. Do not edit by hand; regenerate it.

## What was measured

- 30 fixtures, played at 5 frames per second through the app's own engine (`src/detection/engine.ts`), **one rule at a time**, at each of the three sensitivities.
- **Alarm**: what a person would hear (one per episode). **Event**: a labelled interval in a fixture that a rule is meant to find.
- An alarm is *correct* if it starts inside an event the rule is meant to find (up to 1000 ms after it ends), otherwise it is a *false alarm*. An event is *found* if at least one correct alarm falls in it.
- **Precision** = correct alarms / all alarms. **Recall** = events found / all events. `n/a` means there was nothing to divide by.
- A fixture can count against a rule it was not drawn for: a fingertip at the nose also sits inside the mouth rule's reach, so a nose-picking scene is a false alarm for nail biting. That is real behaviour of the shipped rule, not a bug in the report.
- Recall is flattering: every positive scene is a long, clean hold with a hand that is tracked perfectly. Real footage has dropped detections, fingers hidden behind each other, motion blur and partial hands, none of which these fixtures contain.
- The gate is **medium** sensitivity, the default. A rule marked `validated` in `src/detection/registry.ts` must meet its bar in `scripts/eval-detection/thresholds.json` or the command exits 1. Experimental rules are reported, never gated.

## Known weak spots (drawn on purpose)

- `neg_adjust_glasses_long`: A pinch held at the temple for longer than the dwell looks the same as a pinch at the brow. Only a longer dwell or a real-footage threshold can separate them.
- `neg_cheek_rest_thinking`: Like the chin rest, the fingertips are inside the mouth rule's reach (not the skin-picking rule: nothing moves).
- `neg_chin_rest`: The mouth rule reaches 0.12 of the frame from the lips, so fingertips resting on the jaw are inside it. This is the false alarm the site warns about; WS3 (chin-rest rule) is meant to remove it.
- `neg_drinking`: Fingertips around a glass are inside the mouth rule's reach. Nothing in the landmarks says "glass".
- `neg_eye_rub_brief`: With the index at the eye, the curled little finger of the fist sits about 0.07 from the lips, inside the mouth rule's reach, so nail biting alarms. The eye rule ignores it (no pinch, and 1.2 s is under its dwell).
- `neg_nose_scratch_brief`: A fingertip at the nostril is about 0.05 from the lips, well inside the mouth rule's reach, so nail biting alarms after 0.6 s. The nose rule's longer dwell (1.6 s) correctly ignores it.
- `neg_scratch_cheek`: The cheek is just inside the mouth rule's reach at medium and low sensitivity.
- `neg_yawn_cover_mouth`: A hand over the mouth has fingertips on the lips. The rule cannot tell a yawn from the habit.
