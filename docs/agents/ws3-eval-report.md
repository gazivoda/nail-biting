# WS3 evaluation: fewer false alarms

> **SYNTHETIC. This is not real-world accuracy.**
> 37 landmark fixtures drawn by the same person who wrote the controls (a neutral stick-model face and hand, `scripts/eval-detection/generate-fixtures.mjs`). The chin-rest scenes were drawn to match the rule, so the controls doing well on them shows the rule fires where it was meant to and costs nothing on the synthetic bites. It does not show that real chins, real knuckles or real cameras behave this way. Do not quote these numbers anywhere.

Generated by `npm run eval:detection -- --controls-report docs/agents/ws3-eval-report.md`. Do not edit by hand; regenerate it.

## What is compared

- **shipped**: the detector with the `false_positive_controls` flag OFF. Its decisions are pinned frame by frame against the pre-WS1 code (`src/detection/equivalence.test.ts`).
- **controls**: the flag ON for a person who has not yet tapped "Not a bite": both hands, and the chin-rest rule (the back of the hand, the knuckles or the palm on the chin, and no fingertip within 0.15 face widths of the lips: do not fire).
- **learning**: the flag ON and a simulated person who taps "Not a bite" after every false alarm (judged by the fixture's own labels). Each tap remembers six pose numbers; two like rejections make that pose need a fingertip within 0.2 face widths of the lips. Only the `sequence` fixtures (below) use it: a single scene has no second visit to learn from.
- 35 single-scene fixtures give the headline numbers for shipped and controls; 2 sequence fixtures (one pose five times, then a real bite) are scored apart, so they cannot move the shipped bar.
- Scoring is the harness's: an alarm is *correct* inside a labelled event (up to 1000 ms after it ends), otherwise *false*; an event is *found* by one correct alarm. Precision = correct / all alarms, recall = events found / all events.

## Where the controls make MORE alarms

A veto in the middle of a long hold ends the alarm, and when the hand then comes back to the lips a second alarm can start: one episode becomes two. The alarm time is shorter, but the count is higher.

- `neg_eye_rub_brief` (low): 1 alarm(s) shipped, 2 with the controls.
