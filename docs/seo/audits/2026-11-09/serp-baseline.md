## What this changes

1. **The cancer hypothesis is confirmed.** The query shows an AI Overview that answers "no" and cites HealthTap and UCLA
   Health. Organic results 1 and 3 are the 2018 thumb-amputation stories; ours is fifth and Google shows body text, not
   our meta description. That explains 961 impressions for 1 click. A title change would not fix an answer that already
   sits above the results. Perplexity does cite us first, so the page is trusted by one AI engine.
2. **Perplexity already cites us for acrylics and cancer**, both pages that were rewritten with named sources. The AIO
   sources are often weak (Instagram, Facebook, HealthTap, a dentist's blog), so a well-sourced direct answer can get
   cited; it is not only large institutions.
3. **"Nail biting app" is a store-listing and roundup SERP.** Perplexity's whole answer is seven App Store apps. Stop
   Biting is a browser app and is not in any store, so it cannot appear there. Two roundup articles from August and
   November 2026 (SkinAware, Calm Nails) are the page type we compete with, and our best-apps post lists only two of the
   seven apps AI engines name.
4. **A new smartwatch app, NailGuard, ranks for "smartwatch for nail biting"** and is not in our wearable post. HabitAware's
   Keen2 shows $159 in Google Shopping (the older $199 in our notes is stale or a different listing; verify at source).
5. **Competitor content marketing is accelerating.** nailedapp.io ranks first for "swallow a fingernail" and on page one for
   "grow nails after biting", and a StopBite app blog on habit reversal ranks for query 17. These are the same pages we write.
6. **The head terms (13 to 16, 18, 19) are held by institutions** (AAD, NIH, Cleveland Clinic, Wikipedia, DermNet, WebMD,
   Nationwide Children's). Copy changes will not displace them; the lever is authority plus a distinct angle.
